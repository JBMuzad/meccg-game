Middle-earth CCG (MECCG) is a collectible card game set in Tolkien's Middle-earth. You and your opponent race to accumulate marshalling points by sending companies of characters across the map to recover items, recruit allies and factions, and defeat enemies. Each player chooses an alignment—Wizard, Ringwraith, Fallen-wizard, or Balrog—and constructs a deck from four parts: a play deck, a location deck, a sideboard, and a pool.

New players: Start as a Wizard player using a preconstructed Challenge Deck. Read the Turn Sequence and Movement Rules first. Aim to build companies of 3–6 characters and send them toward sites that let you play your key resources.

Overview

Turn Sequence

Each turn has six phases. The resource player (whose turn it is) plays resource and character cards. The hazard player (the opponent) plays hazard cards during the Movement/Hazard Phase only. (Rules 2.1.1, 2.1.2)

Twilight is the only card playable at any time by either player. It does not count against the hazard limit.
1
Untap Phase
Untap all non-site cards. At a Haven: heal wounded characters to tapped. Hazard player may access hazard sideboard (if opponent's avatar is in play), halving hazard limits for the turn.
2
Organization Phase
Play or discard one character. Reorganize companies (split/join/recompose). Transfer or store items. Tap avatar to access resource sideboard. Declare movement destinations.
3
Long-Event Phase
Discard your resource long-events in play. Play new resource long-events. At end of phase: hazard player discards their hazard long-events.
4
Movement/Hazard Phase
For each company (resource player chooses order): reveal site, set site path and hazard limit, draw cards, resolve passives, hazard player plays hazards, end phase and reset hands to base size.
5
Site Phase
For each company: optionally enter the site, face automatic-attacks and agent/on-guard attacks, then play items/allies/factions at the (now tapped) site. Influence attempts happen here.
6
End-of-Turn Phase
Either player may discard one card. Both players draw or discard to reach base hand size. Resource player signals end of turn; end-of-turn passive conditions resolve.
2.4

When a play deck is exhausted (last card drawn): discard cards that expire on exhaustion; return site cards from discard pile to location deck; exchange up to 5 cards between discard pile and sideboard; shuffle discard pile to form new play deck. This happens immediately when the last card is drawn and cannot be responded to.

All alignments

Pool (Rule 1.7)

1.7

The pool contains up to 10 non-avatar, non-agent characters and up to two non-unique, non-hoard minor items. Character Draft (Rule 1.9)

After declaring alignments, players simultaneously reveal one character from their pool face-down, then reveal. Duplicate unique characters are set aside; all others join the starting company. Stop when: 5 characters (6 for Ringwraith/Balrog), total mind reaches or would exceed 20, or pool is exhausted. Up to 2 minor items from the pool may be placed under up to 2 starting characters.

Starting Sites (Rule 1.10): Wizard: Rivendell (or avatar home site). Ringwraith: Minas Morgul and/or Dol Guldur (up to 2 companies). Fallen-wizard: White Towers or a Ruins & Lairs in Arthedain/Rhudaur. Balrog: Moria and/or Under-gates (up to 2 companies).
1.11 / 1.12

Each player draws up to 8 cards (no mulligan). Starting general influence is 20; Ringwraith and Balrog players have an additional 5 GI that cannot control characters. To determine who goes first, each player rolls 2D6; higher roll goes first (reroll ties).

Phase 1

Untap Phase

At the start of each turn, the resource player untaps their cards and the hazard player has a chance to access their hazard sideboard.

2.I.1

During the untap phase, for each of the resource player's non-site cards: either untap the card if it is tapped, OR if the card is a character at one of the player's havens, heal the character to the tapped position (not fully untapped). Site cards never untap during the untap phase.

All alignments
2.I.2

At the end of the untap phase, the hazard player may (only if the resource player's avatar is in play, and only once per turn): (a) bring up to 5 hazards from their sideboard to their discard pile; OR (b) if their play deck has at least 5 cards, bring 1 hazard from their sideboard directly into their play deck and then shuffle. If the hazard player takes either option, the base hazard limit for each of the resource player's companies is halved (rounded up) for this turn's movement/hazard phases.

Healing vs. untapping: Healing moves a wounded character to the tapped position. It can only happen at a haven during the untap phase. Untapping (from tapped to untapped) happens everywhere. Wounded characters at non-haven sites are simply untapped, not healed.
Phase 2

Organization Phase

The organization phase prepares your characters for the turn. The resource player may perform the following steps in any order, either before or after organizing. (Rule 2.II.1)

Playing or Discarding a Character (Rule 2.II.2)

2.II.2

The resource player may play or discard one character while organizing (only once per turn). An avatar can only be played at the avatar's home site or certain havens. A non-avatar character is played at its home site or one of the player's havens; if the avatar is in play, characters can only be played at the avatar's site or under direct influence with an existing company.

2.II.2.2.3

If a follower is removed from direct influence outside of an organization phase (e.g. its controlling character is eliminated), its mind is not immediately subtracted from general influence. The character must be moved back under general or direct influence during the next organization phase or it is discarded.

Setting Company Composition (Rule 2.II.3)

2.II.3.1

Companies at a haven have no maximum size. Companies not at a haven have a maximum size of 7. Hobbits and Orc scouts count as half a character (rounded up) toward company size. Dúnedain, Dwarves, Elves, and/or Hobbits cannot be in a company with Orcs and/or Trolls (unless at a haven). A company can only have one leader (unless at a haven).

2.II.3.6

A company may be split into multiple companies during the organization phase, but the resulting companies cannot be rejoined during the same organization phase, and all but one of the resulting companies must declare movement to a new site. Any of the resulting companies may move with region movement (rules erratum).

Storing and Transferring Items (Rules 2.II.4, 2.II.5)

2.II.4

A character at a haven may store an item: make a corruption check for the item's bearer; if successful, move the card to the marshalling point pile. Stored cards are no longer borne by the character.

2.II.5

Items may be transferred between two characters at the same site (not necessarily the same company): make a corruption check for the item's bearer. Transfer succeeds as long as the character is still in play after the check.

Accessing Sideboard with Avatar (Rule 2.II.6)

2.II.6

The resource player may tap their avatar to: (a) bring up to 5 resources and/or characters from their sideboard to their discard pile; OR (b) if their play deck has at least 5 cards, bring 1 resource or character from their sideboard directly into their play deck and then shuffle. Card types must be revealed but actual names need not be.

Declaring Movement (Rule 2.II.7)

2.II.7

Place a face-down site card from your location deck with a company to declare movement. Two companies cannot start at the same site of origin and move to the same new site. A company may move to a site card already in play (which then stays in play until end of the company's movement/hazard phase).

2.II.8

At the end of the organization phase, if the total mind of the resource player's characters exceeds their maximum general influence, discard enough non-avatar characters to no longer exceed it. Characters played this turn are returned to hand instead of discarded for this purpose.

Phase 3

Long-Event Phase

Long-events are event cards that remain in play for a single turn cycle (one of your turns plus one of your opponent's turns).

2.III.1

At the beginning of the long-event phase, the resource player immediately discards their own resource long-events from play.

All alignments
2.III.2

The resource player may play new resource long-events during the long-event phase.

2.III.3

At the end of the long-event phase, the hazard player immediately discards their own hazard long-events from play. (The hazard player plays new hazard long-events during the next phase.)

Order matters: First discard existing resource long-events, then play new ones. The classic example: Gates of Morning and Doors of Night alternate control by following this pattern.
Phase 4

Movement/Hazard Phase

In an order chosen by the resource player, each company goes through its own movement/hazard phase. Each proceeds through the following 8 steps (rules 2.IV.i–viii) regardless of whether the company is moving. (Rule 2.IV)

1
Reveal the New Site (2.IV.i)
If moving, the company's face-down site card is revealed. For Under-deeps movement, make the required roll; if failed, the new site is returned and the company stays put (but still has a movement/hazard phase). If movement is illegal when revealed, the movement is negated.
2
Determine the Site Path (2.IV.ii)
The current site becomes the "site of origin." The resource player declares the movement type and site path. Starter Movement uses the path on the site card. Region Movement uses the declared sequence of up to 4 consecutive regions. Under-deeps Movement has no site path. Non-moving companies have no site path.
3
Set the Base Hazard Limit (2.IV.iii)
Base hazard limit = max(2, company size), rounded up. If the hazard player accessed their sideboard during this turn's untap phase, halve the base hazard limit (rounded up).
4
Establish Order of On-going Effects (2.IV.iv)
The hazard player determines the order to apply on-going effects and effects played during the organization phase that depended on the new site or site path.
5
Draw Cards (2.IV.v)
Both players draw simultaneously based on the new site (if moving to a non-haven) or the site of origin (if moving to a haven). Resource player draws up to the lighter-box number (must include an avatar or mind-3+ character). Hazard player draws up to the darker-box number. Both must draw at least 1.
6
Resolve Passive Conditions (2.IV.vi)
Passive conditions triggered at the beginning of the movement/hazard phase are declared in resource player's chosen order. Both players may respond.
7
Play Hazards (2.IV.vii)
The hazard player may play hazards against this company until the hazard limit is reached: creatures (keyed to site path or site type), hazard events, agent hazards, or place one on-guard card on the new site.
8
End the Movement/Hazard Phase (2.IV.viii)
Both players declare done. The site of origin is discarded (if tapped, non-haven) or returned to location deck (if untapped or a haven). Both players reset hands to base hand size by drawing or discarding.
2.IV.iii.1

Each played hazard card counts as one against the hazard limit when it is declared (not when it resolves). Not exceeding the hazard limit is an active condition: there must be fewer declared hazards than the limit at declaration, and no more than the limit at resolution.

2.IV.vii.4

On-guard cards: The hazard player may place one card face-down on the new site as an on-guard card (counts as one against the hazard limit; may be done in response; only once per movement/hazard phase). An on-guard card may be revealed during the site phase under certain conditions.

2.IV.5

At the end of all movement/hazard phases, the resource player must immediately join any companies at the same non-haven site. At a haven, joining is optional.

Game Rules

Movement Rules

Starter Movement (Rule 2.II.7.i)

2.II.7.i

To use Starter Movement, one of the following must be true: (a) The company's current site is a haven listed as the nearest haven on the new site card; (b) the new site is a haven listed as the nearest haven on the current site card; OR (c) both sites are havens with each listing a site path to the other. Wizard players cannot use Starter Movement to/from Gorgoroth. Fallen-wizard and Balrog players cannot use Starter Movement at all.

Region Movement (Rule 2.II.7.ii)

2.II.7.ii

The new site must be within 4 consecutive regions (or up to 6 with a special effect) from the current site, without repeating regions, including both the region of the current site and the region of the new site. The resource player declares the specific path when the new site is revealed.

Rules Erratum: When a company splits, any of the resulting companies may move with region movement. Region movement is not limited to one company after a split.

Under-deeps Movement (Rule 2.II.7.iii)

2.II.7.iii

To use Under-deeps Movement, either the current or new site must be an Under-deeps site, and the new site must be listed as adjacent on the current site card (or vice versa). The company has no site path. If a required roll is greater than 0, the company must make an Under-deeps movement roll; on failure, the new site is returned and the company stays put (this does not count as being returned to site of origin).

Ringwraith Movement Restrictions (Rule 2.II.7.R1)

2.II.7.R1

A company containing a Ringwraith can only move to a non-Darkhaven site if the Ringwraith is in a mode (Black Rider, Fell Rider, or Heralded Lord). Ringwraith companies can only use Starter Movement and cannot have a site path containing a Coastal Sea region. Ringwraith and Balrog players may treat Dagorlad and Ûdun as adjacent.

Playing Creatures (Rule 2.IV.vii.2)

2.IV.vii.2

A creature must be "keyed" when played. The hazard player must specify one of: (a) a region type on the creature's card matching a region in the company's site path; (b) a site type on the creature's card matching the company's new site (or current site if not moving); (c) a specific region name in the site path where the creature is playable per its text; (d) a specific site name matching the company's new or current site per its text.

2.IV.vii.3

Hazard events count as one against the hazard limit and may target the company, its entities, its site path, or the new/current site. Hazard long-events and untargeted hazard permanent-events may also be played.

On-Guard Cards (Rule 2.IV.vii.4)

2.IV.vii.4

The hazard player may place one card face-down on the new site as an on-guard card (once per movement/hazard phase, counts as one against the hazard limit, may be done in response). An on-guard card is revealed during the site phase if it is: a creature keyable to the site (when the site has an automatic-attack); or a hazard event affecting an existing automatic-attack.

Strategy: Smaller companies reduce the hazard limit. A 2-character company draws at most 2 hazards per phase. But smaller companies have less combat strength, so balance both concerns.

Nazgûl Sideboard Access (Rule 2.IV.vii.5)

2.IV.vii.5

The hazard player may tap and discard a Nazgûl permanent-event to bring up to 5 hazards from their sideboard to their discard pile, or (if play deck has 5+ cards) bring 1 hazard from sideboard into play deck and shuffle. This counts as one against the hazard limit.

Phase 5

Site Phase

In an order chosen by the resource player, each company has a site phase. For each company, the resource player may do nothing (ending the site phase immediately) or enter the site. (Rule 2.V)

1
Reveal On-Guard Attacks (2.V.i)
Hazard player may reveal on-guard cards if the card is a creature keyable to the site (and the site has an automatic-attack), or a hazard event affecting an existing automatic-attack at the site.
2
Automatic-Attacks (2.V.ii)
If the site has automatic-attacks, they are initiated one by one in the order listed on the site card. Once all automatic-attacks have been faced (whether or not defeated), the company has successfully entered the site. A character at its home site (if named) may tap to cancel an automatic-attack there.
3
Reveal Agent Attacks (2.V.iii)
Immediately after automatic-attacks, the hazard player may declare that an agent at the company's site will attack (agent must be revealed). Each agent may only attack once per site phase. Agent attacks are not keyed to anything.
4
Resolve On-Guard and Agent Attacks (2.V.iv)
Any declared on-guard creature or agent attacks are initiated and resolved in an order chosen by the resource player.
2.V.1

Playing resources at a site: Allies, factions, and items can only be played during the site phase by an untapped character at an untapped site, tapping both the character and the site when played. The resource player can only take actions during the site phase after successfully entering the site.

2.V.5

Additional minor item: When a resource that taps the site is successfully played, the resource player may immediately play one additional minor item (even if the site doesn't list minor items) by tapping an untapped character.

2.V.6

On-guard cards (site phase): The hazard player may also reveal an on-guard card when the company attempts to play a resource that would tap the site, if the card is a hazard event that would directly affect the company or an influence check, AND the card could have been legally played during the movement/hazard phase. Forbidden: cards that return the company to its site of origin; tap the site; potentially remove a character (except via combat or corruption); force the company to do nothing; or directly tap a company character.

One tapping resource per site visit: Once a resource that taps the site has been played, the site is tapped and no more items/allies/factions can be played there until the site resets (when the play deck is exhausted and re-shuffled).
Phase 6

End-of-Turn Phase

The turn ends in three steps. (Rule 2.VI)

2.VI.i

Step 1: Either player may discard one card from their hand.

All alignments
2.VI.ii

Step 2: Both players reset their hands by drawing or discarding to their base hand size.

2.VI.iii

Step 3: The resource player signals the end of the turn. All end-of-turn passive conditions are declared and resolved in resource player's chosen order. No other actions may be taken during this step unless specifically allowed at end of turn.

Cards may be played during the end-of-turn phase after hand size has been reconciled (between steps 2 and 3).
Combat & Risk

Combat

Combat is initiated when a hazard creature resolves, when entering a site with automatic-attacks, or during Company vs. Company Combat. The resolution of a declared attack proceeds through 5 steps. (Rule 3)

1
Pre-Assignment Actions (3.i)
Either player may take actions to cancel the attack or modify attributes of the attack as a whole (number of strikes, prowess, body, who assigns strikes, etc.). Once strikes are assigned, the attack can no longer be cancelled.
2
Defending Player Assigns Strikes (3.ii)
The defending player assigns untapped characters to strikes. The defending player may defer assigning one or more strikes even if untapped characters are available.
3
Opponent Assigns Remaining Strikes (3.iii)
The attacking player assigns any remaining strikes to unassigned characters (up to the number of characters facing the attack). Excess strikes cannot be cancelled but need not be defeated for the attack to be defeated.
4
Strike Sequences (3.iv)
In an order chosen by the defending player, perform a Strike Sequence for each assigned strike. Players may take actions before, between, and after strike sequences (as allowed in the current phase).
5
Resolve the Attack (3.v)
Creature: if all assigned strikes defeated, creature goes to defending player's MP pile. If any strike not defeated: creature is discarded. Automatic-attacks: if all assigned strikes defeated, the attack is defeated. Agent attacks: if all strikes defeated and all body checks fail, a Hero/Fallen-wizard player may take the agent as kill MPs.

3.i.5

Effects that allow a character to face multiple strikes must be declared before strikes are assigned. If a character is eliminated by one of its strikes, any remaining unresolved strikes assigned to it are considered successful.

3.v.W1/R1

When a Wizard or Fallen-wizard player defeats a creature with a * next to its marshalling points, the creature is removed from play (not placed in the MP pile). When a Ringwraith or Balrog player defeats a creature without a * next to its MPs, the creature is removed from play instead.

Combat & Risk

Body Checks

A body check is made when a character is wounded (fails a strike) or when a creature is defeated.

3.I.1

To resolve a body check: the player who does NOT control the entity rolls 2D6 and applies any modifications (including +1 if the entity was already wounded before the strike). If the modified roll is higher than the entity's body, the entity fails the body check and is eliminated.

Combat & Risk

Detainment

3.II.2

If an attack is detainment: a successful strike taps the character instead of wounding them; a failed strike results in no body check (the strike simply fails). No body checks are ever initiated from detainment attacks.

All alignments
3.II.3

Detainment creature attacks are worth 0 marshalling points. If all strikes of a detainment creature attack were assigned and defeated, the creature is removed from play (not placed in the MP pile).

3.II.R1

A Ringwraith player treats as detainment: any attack keyed to a Dark-domain, Dark-hold, Shadow-hold, or Darkhaven (including keyed by name to such a region or site); any Orc, Troll, Undead, or Man attack keyed to Shadow-land; and all agent attacks against their companies.

RingwraithBalrog
3.II.5

A Nazgûl attack against a minion company is detainment, unless the attack is an automatic-attack (which always attacks normally).

Key consequence: With detainment, there are no wounds and no body checks. Cards that trigger special effects when a character is wounded have no effect during detainment strikes.
Combat & Risk

Strike Sequence

Once strikes are assigned, the defending player chooses the order to resolve them. For each assigned strike, a Strike Sequence is performed (Annotation 18 / Rule 3.iv). Players may take actions between strike sequences as allowed by the current phase.

1
Attacking Player Actions (3.iv.1)
If during the opponent's movement/hazard phase, the hazard player may take hazard actions affecting the strike (still counts against hazard limit).
2
Allocate Excess Strikes (3.iv.2)
The non-defending player may allocate unallocated excess strikes as −1 modifications to the prowess of the character facing this strike. On the last strike, all remaining excess strikes must be allocated.
3
Apply −3 to Stay Untapped (3.iv.3)
The defending player may apply −3 to the character's prowess to prevent that character from tapping after the strike (whether the strike succeeds or fails).
4
Tap for +1 Support (3.iv.4)
The defending player may tap one or more untapped characters in the same company who haven't been assigned a strike. Each tapped character applies +1 to the prowess of the character facing the strike.
5
Defending Player Actions (3.iv.5)
The defending player may take resource/character actions affecting the strike, including playing up to one resource per character that requires a skill. A strike may be cancelled up until the dice-roll is made (even if the attack itself could not be cancelled).
6
Roll 2D6 and Resolve (3.iv.6 & 3.iv.7)
The defending player rolls 2D6 and adds the character's prowess after all modifications. If roll > strike prowess: strike fails; character taps (unless −3 taken); body check against the creature. If roll < strike prowess: strike succeeds; character is immediately wounded; body check against the character. If roll = strike prowess: strike is ineffectual; character taps (unless −3 taken).
One skill resource per character per strike: Up to one resource that requires a skill may be played per character per strike during step 5.

Direct Influence (DI) (Rule 2.II.2.2)

DI

Some characters have direct influence (the black hand symbol on a character card). A character may control another character as a follower if the character's DI is at least equal to the follower's mind. Followers' minds are subtracted from their controller's DI (not from GI). Followers cannot have followers.

2.II.2.2.4

Direct influence restricted to certain races/cards only applies once (not per character). When DI would be reduced, unrestricted DI is reduced before restricted DI.

Influence Attempts (Rule 8)

8.1

During the site phase, a resource player may declare an influence attempt against an opponent's ally, character, faction, or item by tapping a character (active condition), subject to: not the player's first turn; the company has entered its site; no prior influence attempt or CvCC attack this turn; the target is not an avatar or controlled by an avatar. For items, the player must also reveal an identical item from hand.

8.3

Resolving: Roll 2D6 + influencing character's unused DI − opponent's unused GI − opponent's roll (2D6) − controlling character's unused DI (if any). Compare to target's mind (or faction requirement). If result > that value, the influence attempt succeeds and the target is discarded.

8.W1

Wizard players suffer −5 to influence rolls against Ringwraith or Balrog player cards (and vice versa). Fallen-wizard players suffer −5 against Ringwraith or Balrog player cards.

Game Rules

Corruption

7.1

To resolve a corruption check: the character's player rolls 2D6 and applies modifications. The result is compared to the character's corruption point total (sum of all corruption points on cards the character controls):

  • Roll > corruption total: check succeeds, no effect.
  • Roll = or one less than corruption total: Hero character is discarded; Wizard avatar is eliminated; Minion character or Fallen-wizard avatar only taps (check counts as successful).
  • Roll < two below corruption total: character fails and is eliminated; all non-follower cards discarded.
All alignments
7.1.1

Other characters in the same company may tap (one at a time, each as a separate action) to apply +1 to the corruption check roll per character tapped. This cannot be done during an opponent's turn.

7.1.R1 / 7.1.B1

A character in the same company as a Ringwraith or Balrog receives an additional +2 modifier to corruption checks.

7.2.1

Only one corruption card may be played on each character per turn. A corruption card (keyword "corruption") can only be played when doing so would initiate a new chain of effects.

7.4

Allies, Ringwraiths, and Balrogs are not affected by corruption and never make corruption checks.

Free Council: At the end of the game, all non-Ringwraith, non-Balrog characters make a final corruption check. Manage your characters' corruption totals carefully throughout the game.

Removing Corruption Cards (Rule 7.3)

7.3

When attempting to remove a corruption card that requires tapping a character to make a roll, the player may apply a −3 modification to the roll to not tap the character. This cannot be done if an attempt to remove the same corruption card was made this turn, and no more untapped attempts may be made for the rest of the turn once one untapped attempt has been made.

Game Rules

Allies

2.V.2

To play an ally: the company must have successfully entered the site; the site must be untapped; the resource player taps an untapped character (active condition). The ally is placed under that character's control and the site taps. Playing an ally is not an influence attempt; the ally's mind is not subtracted from the controller's DI.

All alignments
2.V.2.2

Allies are not characters, but are treated as characters for: combat-specific actions (facing strikes, tapping to support or cancel attacks, being affected by cards referring to characters during combat); "skill only" cards or effects; and healing.

2.V.2.3

If an ally's controlling character leaves play, the ally is immediately discarded.

6.1.6

Allies cannot bear or use items.

Ally MP rules: Ally marshalling points only count at the Free Council if the ally is still in play. "Discard if he/she moves to [region/site]" means the ally is discarded at the beginning of the movement/hazard phase when that movement is declared.
Game Rules

Factions

2.V.3

To play a faction: the company has entered the site; the site is untapped; an untapped character taps (active condition). The resource player reveals the faction, then makes an influence check: roll 2D6 + character's available DI + any other modifications. If result > faction's requirement, the faction is played into the MP pile and the site taps. Otherwise the faction is discarded.

All alignments
Faction std

Reference

Glossary

Action
A one-time activity that changes the board state when performed (e.g. drawing a card, rolling dice, moving a company, tapping/discarding/playing a card, etc.).
Agent
A card that may be played as a hazard (or as a character by Ringwraith/Fallen-wizard players). Agents can move, attack, and make influence attempts. Total mind of all agents in a deck cannot exceed 36.
Ally
A resource representing a non-character personality (e.g. Tom Bombadil, Treebeard). Played during the site phase by tapping a character; taps the site. Allies are not characters except for combat, skill-only effects, and healing purposes.
Automatic-Attack
An attack listed on a site card that must be faced in order to enter the site. Cannot be avoided by Stealth or Secret Entrance. A character at its (named) home site may tap to cancel it.
Avatar
The Wizard, Ringwraith, Fallen-wizard, or Balrog card representing the player in the game. If eliminated: −5 miscellaneous MPs, no new avatar may be played.
Body Check
A 2D6 roll made by the non-controlling player against an entity's body. If the modified roll > body: entity is eliminated. Body checks resolve immediately after wounds/defeats.
Chain of Effects
A sequence of declared (but unresolved) actions that resolves in reverse order of declaration (Last In, First Out). The resource player always has priority to initiate a new chain.
Company
A group of characters moving and acting together at a single site. Maximum size 7 at non-haven sites (Hobbits/Orc scouts count as half). No maximum at havens.
Corruption Check
A 2D6 roll compared to a character's corruption point total. Result > total: success. Result = total or one less: Hero discarded, Wizard eliminated, Minion taps. Result < by 2+: character eliminated.
Corruption Points (CP)
Points on cards a character controls that form the corruption point total. Items, some hazards, and other cards can add CPs. The One Ring adds substantial corruption.
Darkhaven
A Minion haven site (the Ringwraith/Balrog equivalent of a Hero haven). Minas Morgul and Dol Guldur are the primary starting Darkhavens for Ringwraith players; Moria and Under-gates for Balrog.
Detainment
An attack type where successful strikes tap characters instead of wounding them. No body checks are ever made from detainment strikes. Detainment creatures yield no MPs when defeated.
Direct Influence (DI)
A character attribute (black hand symbol) used to control followers, make influence attempts, and recruit factions. A character can control a follower whose mind ≤ the character's available DI.
Discard Pile
The pile of discarded cards beside the play deck. When the play deck is exhausted, the discard pile (after sideboard exchange and returning site cards) is shuffled to form the new play deck.
Exhausted Deck
When the last card is drawn from the play deck: return site cards to location deck, exchange up to 5 cards with sideboard, then shuffle the discard pile into the new play deck. This happens immediately.
Faction
A resource representing a large group or unique Dragon that can be recruited. Played via an influence check during the site phase. Factions must remain in play (not stored) to count MPs.
Fallen-wizard
A corrupted Wizard who has strayed from the Free Peoples' cause. Plays a mix of Hero and Minion cards; uses Stage resources; covert/overt company status. Introduced in MEWH (1997).
Follower
A character controlled by another character's direct influence. The follower's mind is deducted from the controlling character's DI rather than from the player's GI. Followers cannot have followers.
Free Council
The end-game procedure for Wizard players. When the game ends: all non-Ringwraith/non-Balrog characters make corruption checks, then MPs are totaled, doubled/halved as needed, duplicates revealed, and avatar penalties applied.
General Influence (GI)
A pool of 20 points (25 for Ringwraith/Balrog, of which 5 cannot control characters) used to control non-follower characters. Available GI = GI − sum of non-follower character minds; always stops at zero.
Hand Size
Number of cards a player holds. Base hand size is 8. Reset at end of each movement/hazard phase and end-of-turn phase. Effects may modify base hand size cumulatively.
Haven
A safe site for Hero characters (Rivendell, Lórien, etc.). Characters heal at havens during the untap phase. Items may be stored at havens. Companies at havens have no size limit.
Hazard Limit
The maximum number of hazard cards the hazard player may play against a company per movement/hazard phase: max(2, company size), rounded up. Halved (rounded up) if the hazard player accessed their sideboard during the untap phase.
Hoard
A designation indicating that certain items are playable at a site. Any site that has a Dragon automatic-attack at the beginning of a turn contains a hoard. Defeated Dragon manifestations remove the hoard.
Long-event
An event card that stays in play for one full turn cycle (one rotation of all players' turns). Resource long-events are discarded at the start of the resource player's long-event phase.
Manifestation
A mechanically different version of the same entity. Only one manifestation of a unique entity can be in play at a time (except Dragon manifestations). Avatar manifestations may be played even if other manifestations of the same avatar exist in play.
Marshalling Points (MP)
Victory points. Goal: 25 MP (Short game). Sources: character MPs, ally MPs, item MPs, faction MPs, kill MPs (defeated creatures), miscellaneous MPs. Eliminated avatar: −5 MP.
Mind
Character attribute (white head symbol) indicating how much GI or DI is needed to control the character. Also used in influence attempts against characters and allies.
On-Guard Card
A hazard card placed face-down on a site during the movement/hazard phase (counts 1 against hazard limit). May be revealed during the site phase if specific conditions are met.
Permanent-event
An event card that stays in play until discarded by a rule or effect. Can only be played if it would have an immediate or potential effect on the game. Discarded immediately if all characters in its company leave play.
Pool
Up to 10 non-avatar characters (and 2 minor items) set aside before the game for the character draft. Unused pool characters may be shuffled into the play deck or removed from the game.
Prowess
The combat strength of a character, creature, or attack. Used in strike resolution: defender rolls 2D6 + prowess vs. the attack's prowess.
Removed-from-play
A pile for eliminated avatars and other cards permanently removed from the game. Cards with * MPs go here when defeated by Wizard players instead of to the MP pile.
Short-event
An event card that resolves and is immediately discarded. Must have some immediate effect on the board state (cannot be played just to discard it).
Sideboard
A reserve set of 30–40 cards accessed during the game. Hazard player may access during opponent's untap phase (halves hazard limits). Resource player may access by tapping avatar. Up to 5 cards exchanged with discard pile on deck exhaustion.
Site of Origin
The site a company is departing from during its movement/hazard phase. Once the new site is revealed, the company is "between" sites until the site of origin is removed at the end of the movement/hazard phase.
Site Path
The sequence of regions between a company's current site and new site, as determined by the movement type used. Determines which creature hazards are playable against the company.
Stage Resource
Fallen-wizard-specific permanent-events that unlock additional capabilities (e.g. playing Orc characters). Exactly 3 stage points totaling to 3 must be in the Fallen-wizard pool at game start. Only playable during the organization phase.
Strike
An individual attack within a larger attack. Resolved through the Strike Sequence (steps 1–6 per 3.iv). An attack may have multiple strikes.
Tap / Untap
Tapping a card (rotating 90°) marks it as used. Untapping (rotating back) happens at the start of the untap phase for all non-site cards. Site cards never untap via the untap phase; they reset only when the play deck is exhausted.
Trophy
A defeated creature card held by an Orc or Troll character that faced one of its strikes. Treated as a minor item worth 0 CP; provides prowess/DI bonuses based on total printed MP value. Cannot be transferred or stored.
Twilight
The only card playable at any time by either player as either a resource or a hazard. Never counts against the hazard limit. The sole exception to phase-based play restrictions.
Under-deeps
A site category deep beneath Middle-earth. Introduced in MEBA. Movement between adjacent Under-deeps sites requires a roll; failure keeps the company at its current site (not treated as returning to site of origin).
Unique
A card type of which only one copy may exist in play simultaneously (across both players). Max 1 copy per deck (except avatars). Only face-up agents count for uniqueness checks.
Wound / Wounded
A character state (the card is inverted/rotated 180°) resulting from a successful strike. Wounded characters receive −2 prowess in combat. Can only heal at a haven during the untap phase (moves to tapped). Triggers a body check immediately after wounding.

Free Council Modifications (Rules 10.3.iii–vi)

10.3.iii

Doubling: If a player has zero or fewer MPs in a source (other than Kill or Miscellaneous), their opponent's MPs from that source are doubled.

10.3.iv

Half rule: If more than half of a player's total MPs come from a single source (ignoring negative sources), that player reduces that source until it equals no more than half their total.

10.3.v

Duplicate reveal: Each player may reveal cards from their hand that match unique cards giving the opponent MPs. For each card revealed, the opponent's total is reduced by 1.

10.3.vi

Avatar penalty: −5 MPs applied after all other modifications if a player's avatar was eliminated.

Timing & Actions

Chain of Effects (Rule 9.5)

9.5

A chain of effects begins when a new action is declared. Before it resolves, either player may respond by declaring their own action. Once neither player wishes to respond, the sequence resolves in reverse order of declaration (Last In, First Out). The resource player always has priority to initiate a new chain of effects.

All alignments
9.5.3

If a rule or effect happens "immediately," it resolves without initiating a chain of effects and without either player being allowed to respond.

Active Conditions (Rule 9.2)

9.2

Active conditions are requirements for an action to be declared and resolved. Tapping a card as an active condition is synonymous with the declaration itself (not a separate action). If any active condition is no longer valid upon resolution, unresolved effects are negated but the conditions already performed remain.

Passive Conditions (Rule 9.6)

9.6

When a passive condition comes into effect during a chain of effects, the resulting action(s) are declared in a subsequent chain that immediately follows the current chain resolving. Passive conditions that would result in a card being discarded resolve immediately (not in a subsequent chain).

9.6.4

Passive conditions triggered at the "beginning" of a phase must be declared in a single chain before any other actions during that phase. Passive conditions at the "end" of a phase must be declared in a single chain once both players declare they are finished taking actions.

Key Annotations

Ann. 12

An attack is resolved and concluded when the final strike, all special actions from the final strike, and the associated body check are resolved.

Ann. 13

An attack may not be cancelled once its strikes have been assigned. A strike may not be cancelled once the dice-roll for the strike has been made.

Ann. 14

A cancelled attack has no effect on its target company, except that the company is considered to have faced the attack in its history.

Ann. 18

During a Strike Sequence, once the defending player chooses to resolve a strike, the only actions that may be taken until the dice-roll are: hazard actions affecting the strike; the attacker allocating −1 modifications from excess strikes; the defending character taking a −3 to stay untapped; the defending character playing resources that affect the strike.

Ann. 22

When a character fails a body check, items may be immediately reassigned to unwounded characters (one per character). This reassignment is synonymous with the failed body check — no actions may take place in between. Unassigned items are discarded.

Ann. 25

A company is considered to be at its site card at all times except from the moment the new site card is revealed during the movement/hazard phase until the old site card is discarded at the end of the same movement/hazard phase.

Twilight timing: Twilight is the only card playable at any time by either player. It never counts against the hazard limit and may be played in response to any action in any chain of effects.
